Six task families, each a different visual relation

Analog gauge reading

Reading plant instruments from site photos / CCTV (pressure, temperature, flow).

Fine angular estimation + interpolation between tick marks + scale arithmetic

Report chart reading

Extracting numbers from charts in PDFs, dashboards and scanned reports.

Reading values against axis gridlines, ranking near-equal bars, arithmetic over read values

Wire tracing

Verifying wiring / piping diagrams, patch panels and control-cabinet photos.

Continuous contour following through crossings (a known weak spot of patch-based vision)

Conjunction counting

Counting parts on trays, items on shelves, defects on boards (inventory & QC).

Exact enumeration under clutter; binding colour+shape (conjunction search)

Spatial relations

Layout checks: planogram compliance, site-plan / floor-plan questions, robot pick targets.

Metric distance comparison, relational counting and ordering by position

Route planning

Floor-plan / warehouse navigation: shortest route between two marked locations.

Multi-step spatial planning with global constraints (walls), path length counting

Scoring that cleanly separates correct from incorrect

Rules

  • No multiple choice. Answers are numbers, integers or object names, so guessing almost never works (chance is 12% per task, ~0% per pair).
  • Tolerance = 0.45 × the value resolution (e.g. a minor tick). Any reading that lands on the neighbouring tick is wrong.
  • Strict format: the last line must be ANSWER: …. Unparsed answers count as wrong and are reported separately.
  • Headline = pair accuracy: both twins must be right.
  • Visual sensitivity: did the model change its answer when the decisive detail changed?

Pair accuracy (both twins correct) per family and level · 57 pairs · 115 of 120 tasks evaluated within the free-tier daily token quota · model served on Groq.

  • Sharp split by skill: Report chart reading 20/20 · Wire tracing 14/15 · Spatial relations 14/20 · Analog gauge reading 9/20 · Route planning 1/20 · Conjunction counting 0/20 tasks correct.
  • Counting hallucination: 9/11 parsed counting errors are over-counts, even with only 1–3 objects on a white background; the model's reasoning invents “partially visible” objects at the edges (4 responses).
  • Gauges break at one knob: 9/12 correct at L0–L2, 0/8 at L3–L4. Once only every other major tick is labelled, readings drift by one or two scale steps (e.g. 45 read as 60). The red set-point decoy is never mistaken for the pointer.
  • Runaway reasoning: 26/115 responses (13 of 20 mazes) loop until the 2,048-token cap and never commit an answer, so they are scored unparsed and never guessed. Route planning: 1/20 correct.
  • Visual sensitivity 90% on evaluated pairs: Qwen does look at the image — its failures are perception errors, not text priors (text-only controls: 0% pairs).

Engineering judgement and limitations

Design choices

  • Procedural rendering: exact ground truth, no GPU, near-zero cost.
  • Pixel verifiers, not a model judge: free of model bias.
  • Counterfactual pairs: a structural guarantee that the image is needed.
  • Cost: 176,058 input + 84,659 output tokens on Groq's free tier; cached re-runs are free and --budget caps calls.

Limitations

  • Clean synthetic renders give a lower bound on real-photo difficulty.
  • Verifiers know the rendering conventions (palette, dial geometry).
  • One fixed prompt per task; scores may shift slightly with other prompts.
  • Two twin pairs per cell: results are indicative; scale up with the generator.

Impact · a trustworthy gate for vision models in operations

Model selection

Know where a model breaks (dial density, clutter, crossings) before trusting it on plant photos, reports or diagrams.

Regression testing

Seeded, deterministic sets act as a CI gate. A new model release must not lower pair accuracy on any family.

Data for improvement

The Failure-Frontier hard set is verified, labelled training and evaluation data aimed at each model's weak spots.
